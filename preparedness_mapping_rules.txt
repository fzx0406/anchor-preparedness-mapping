Country and preparedness-exposure mapping rules

Definitions

1. occurrence_countries_strict
   Countries in which the triggering risk event explicitly occurred.
   This field represents reported risk-origin geography.

2. directly_affected_countries
   Countries for which the source explicitly reports an operational,
   commercial, regulatory, logistics, or supply-chain consequence.
   This field represents documented-exposure geography.

3. shipping_network_affected_countries
   Countries that are relevant for preparedness because the source
   establishes a shipping, rerouting, chokepoint, port-substitution,
   trade-lane, or trade-flow relationship with the reported event.
   This field represents preparedness-exposure geography.

Preparedness exposure is an analytical scenario relation.
It does not mean that disruption, loss, or propagation was actually observed
in the included country.


General country rules

- Use standard English country names only, e.g. United States, China,
  Russia, United Kingdom, South Korea.

- Do not output ports, cities, terminals, canals, straits, seas, oceans,
  continents, or regions as countries.

- Do not output labels such as Europe, Asia, Global, Middle East,
  Red Sea, Suez Canal, Panama Canal, or Strait of Hormuz in any country list.

- occurrence_countries_strict must be narrow.
  Include a country only when the source explicitly locates the triggering
  event in that country.

- directly_affected_countries must also be source-supported.
  Include a country only when the source explicitly states that the country,
  its national market, its ports, its shipping services, or its trade flows
  experienced an operational, commercial, regulatory, or supply-chain effect.

- shipping_network_affected_countries may be broader than
  directly_affected_countries, but every additional country must satisfy
  at least one preparedness-exposure rule P1--P5 below.

- Do not add countries solely because they are economically important,
  geographically close, major maritime nations, large trading economies,
  or generally connected to global shipping.

- Do not infer an unrestricted list of countries from regional expressions
  such as "Europe", "Asia", "Middle East", or "global shipping".

- A regional expression may support preparedness exposure only for countries
  that are also identifiable from the same source text through a specific
  route, trade, rerouting, port, or market relationship.

- If a port or city is mentioned, output the corresponding country only when
  the country can be established from information contained in the source
  record. Do not output the port or city itself.

- If no defensible country can be identified for a field, return ["Unknown"].

- Remove duplicate countries.

- Return no more than 20 countries in
  shipping_network_affected_countries.

- Every country included in shipping_network_affected_countries must have:
  (1) at least one preparedness-exposure label;
  (2) at least one corresponding rule ID;
  (3) a short explanation of the shipping logic;
  (4) source-supported evidence or source context.


Preparedness-exposure labels

Each country in shipping_network_affected_countries must be assigned
one or more of the following preparedness-exposure labels:

P1 -> trade_lane_exposure
P2 -> rerouting_exposure
P3 -> chokepoint_exposure
P4 -> port_substitution_exposure
P5 -> trade_flow_exposure

If more than one rule applies to the same country, include the country only
once in shipping_network_affected_countries, but retain all applicable
preparedness-exposure labels and rule IDs.


P1. Trade-lane exposure
preparedness_exposure label: trade_lane_exposure

Apply P1 when the source identifies an affected maritime trade lane,
shipping service, origin-destination route, liner corridor, tanker route,
dry-bulk route, container route, or other identifiable shipping connection.

Include a country under P1 only if at least one of the following is true:

(a) the country is explicitly identified as an origin of the affected route;
(b) the country is explicitly identified as a destination of the affected route;
(c) the source explicitly connects the country's ports, cargo, exporters,
    importers, carriers, or national market to the affected route;
(d) the country is explicitly identified as part of the shipping service
    or trade-lane context described in the source.

Do not add countries merely because a general regional route such as
"Asia-Europe trade" is mentioned.

If the source only names regions and provides no country-level information,
do not generate country assignments under P1.


P2. Rerouting exposure
preparedness_exposure label: rerouting_exposure

Apply P2 only when the source explicitly describes rerouting, diversion,
avoidance, route extension, route switching, alternative routing,
or the use of another maritime corridor.

First identify from the source:

(a) the disrupted route, waterway, corridor, or shipping service; and
(b) the alternative route or rerouting response, when stated.

Include a country under P2 only when the source establishes that the country:

(a) is an origin or destination of the rerouted service;
(b) has ports or shipping services explicitly involved in the rerouting;
(c) is explicitly described as receiving diverted traffic;
(d) is explicitly described as facing additional voyage distance,
    delay, capacity pressure, freight effects, or operational adjustment
    because of the rerouting.

Do not add countries simply because an event causes vessels to reroute
around a major maritime area.

Do not infer all countries that could theoretically use the affected route.

If the source establishes rerouting but no country-level relation can be
identified, do not create additional country assignments under P2.


P3. Chokepoint exposure
preparedness_exposure label: chokepoint_exposure

Apply P3 when the source identifies disruption, restriction, congestion,
closure, security risk, low-water conditions, or another operational problem
at a canal, strait, maritime chokepoint, or constrained waterway.

Include a country under P3 only when the source establishes a country-level
relationship with that chokepoint, such as:

(a) the country's imports or exports use the affected chokepoint;
(b) the country's ports or shipping services are explicitly linked to it;
(c) the country's vessels, cargo flows, or markets are explicitly described
    as being exposed to the chokepoint disruption;
(d) the source explicitly reports a national operational or preparedness
    implication arising from the chokepoint disruption.

Do not convert the geographical location of a chokepoint directly into
a broad list of potentially dependent countries.

The country in which a canal, strait, or waterway is geographically located
does not automatically become a preparedness-exposure country unless it also
satisfies one of the above conditions. Its role as an occurrence country
should be handled separately in occurrence_countries_strict.


P4. Port-substitution exposure
preparedness_exposure label: port_substitution_exposure

Apply P4 when the source describes port substitution, cargo diversion,
alternative-port use, traffic shifting, replacement port capacity,
or another response in which cargo or vessel calls move from one port
to another.

Include a country under P4 only when:

(a) an alternative or substitute port in that country is explicitly named;
(b) the source explicitly states that traffic, vessels, or cargo were shifted
    to a port in that country;
(c) the source explicitly identifies that country's port system as an
    alternative for the disrupted location;
(d) the source explicitly links the country to a substitution response
    resulting from the disruption.

For a domestic port disruption, do not automatically include major foreign
trading partners.

Do not infer alternative ports solely from geographical proximity,
port size, or general maritime knowledge.

If the source discusses possible port substitution but does not identify
a country-level alternative, do not generate an additional country under P4.


P5. Trade-flow or shipping-market exposure
preparedness_exposure label: trade_flow_exposure

Apply P5 when the source describes effects on international trade flows,
container flows, dry-bulk flows, tanker flows, cargo allocation,
shipping capacity, freight rates, insurance conditions, or maritime markets.

Include a country under P5 only when the source explicitly establishes that:

(a) the country is an origin or destination of the affected trade flow;
(b) the country's exporters or importers are linked to the affected flow;
(c) the country's shipping market or port system is linked to the
    described capacity or freight-rate effect;
(d) a bilateral or multilateral trade relation involving that country is
    explicitly identified in the source.

A general statement such as "global freight rates increased",
"world trade was affected", or "Asian markets were disrupted"
is not sufficient to add individual countries.

Do not expand general market effects into country lists unless the source
provides country-level support.


Relationship between the three country layers

A country may appear in more than one geographical layer.

Case 1:
If the triggering event occurs in country A,
include A in occurrence_countries_strict.

Case 2:
If the source explicitly states that country B experienced a consequence,
include B in directly_affected_countries.

Case 3:
If country C is not explicitly described as having experienced a realised
effect, but the source establishes one of the P1--P5 preparedness relations,
include C in shipping_network_affected_countries only.

Do not move a preparedness-exposure country into
directly_affected_countries unless the source explicitly documents an effect.

Do not interpret shipping_network_affected_countries as evidence
of realised cross-border transmission.


Multiple-rule handling

- Evaluate P1--P5 independently.

- A country may satisfy more than one preparedness rule.

- If a country satisfies multiple rules, list the country only once in
  shipping_network_affected_countries.

- Retain all applicable preparedness-exposure labels and rule IDs
  for that country.

Example:
If a country is both an endpoint of an affected trade lane and explicitly
affected by rerouting, assign:

labels:
["trade_lane_exposure", "rerouting_exposure"]

rule_ids:
["P1", "P2"]


Maximum-country rule

If 20 or fewer countries satisfy P1--P5, retain all qualifying countries.

If more than 20 countries satisfy P1--P5, retain countries according to
the following evidence priority:

Priority 1:
Countries explicitly named in the source as having a route, rerouting,
substitution, chokepoint, or trade-flow relationship.

Priority 2:
Countries whose ports, exporters, importers, carriers, cargo flows,
or national markets are explicitly connected to the event in the source.

Priority 3:
Countries supported only through broader source-described regional
shipping or trade context.

Within the same priority level, preserve the order in which the supporting
country relations appear in the source.

Do not rank countries by GDP, trade volume, political importance,
port size, or general maritime importance.

Set truncated = true whenever qualifying countries are removed because
of the 20-country limit.


Unknown handling

Return ["Unknown"] only when no defensible country can be assigned
to the relevant field.

Do not use ["Unknown"] together with valid country names in the same field.

For preparedness exposure, if the event clearly involves a network mechanism
but no country-level relation can be established from the source,
return ["Unknown"] and explain the reason in unknown_reason.


Evidence requirements

For every country included in shipping_network_affected_countries,
record:

- country
- preparedness_exposure label or labels
- rule_id or rule_ids
- trigger_type
- short shipping logic
- supporting source phrase or source-supported context

The explanation must identify why the country was included.
Do not provide a generic explanation that could apply to any country.

Good:
"Rule P2: Germany is included because the source states that
Asia-Europe services to German ports were rerouted around the Cape."

Bad:
"Germany is important to global shipping."


Required output structure

{
  "occurrence_countries_strict": [],
  "directly_affected_countries": [],
  "shipping_network_affected_countries": [],

  "preparedness_exposure": [
    {
      "country": "",
      "labels": [],
      "rule_ids": [],
      "trigger_type": [],
      "evidence": "",
      "source_phrase": ""
    }
  ],

  "scenario_type": [],

  "rule_trace": [],

  "truncated": false,

  "truncated_countries": [],

  "unknown_reason": ""
}


Final self-check before output

Before returning the result, verify all of the following:

1. Every occurrence country is explicitly supported as the event location.

2. Every directly affected country has an explicitly documented consequence.

3. Every preparedness-exposure country satisfies at least one rule P1--P5.

4. Every preparedness-exposure country has at least one corresponding
   preparedness-exposure label.

5. Every preparedness-exposure country has at least one rule ID.

6. No country was added solely from general world knowledge,
   economic importance, geographic proximity, or subjective relevance.

7. No port, city, canal, sea, region, continent, or "Global" label appears
   in a country field.

8. Regional expressions have not been freely converted into country lists.

9. Duplicate countries have been removed.

10. The number of shipping_network_affected_countries does not exceed 20.

11. If more than 20 countries qualified, the stated evidence-priority rule
    was applied and truncated was set to true.

12. No preparedness-exposure relation is described as realised propagation
    unless the source explicitly documents that consequence.
